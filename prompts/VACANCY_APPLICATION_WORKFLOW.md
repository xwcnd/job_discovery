# ONE-VACANCY APPLICATION WORKFLOW — SHAREABLE TEMPLATE

> **Placement:** Use this prompt in a NEW private chat for one position, inside the job seeker's own ChatGPT Project, after a job has been discovered or selected. This is a second-stage application workflow, **not** the daily Job Discovery alert automation. Store real CVs/project records in the user's private Project or Drive, never in this repository.
> **Personalization:** Contact information, work authorization, signature names, output folder links, styling and career facts must come only from the current user's private sources. Never inherit the template author's identity or facts. Respect the explicit approval checkpoints before CV drafting and before cover-letter creation.

NEW JOB APPLICATION — VACANCY WORKFLOW


This chat is dedicated to ONE vacancy.


All Project Instructions remain mandatory, especially source priority, factual integrity, whole-document quality, anti-loss/regression, natural-human writing, document formatting, ATS/form handling, and consistency.


Use the complete factual Project source set, not only previous tailored outputs.


The JD defines what the employer requires. Project factual sources define what I have actually done.


Do not restart the vacancy analysis unless new information materially changes it.


MANDATORY DOCUMENT FORMAT AND DELIVERY


By default, if the candidate has connected Google Drive and selected this delivery workflow, the only saved CV and cover-letter deliverables should be editable, native Google Docs in the private Drive folder specified for the vacancy. A PDF or DOCX uploaded to Drive is not a native Google Doc. If native Docs creation is unavailable or the user selects another format, explain the limitation and obtain the user's decision; do not claim that a Google Doc was created.


When using native Docs, provide the verified Google Docs link in chat together with the requested short QA summary. Do not upload, save, archive or otherwise retain generated PDF or DOCX copies in Google Drive, ChatGPT Library or any other persistent destination. Do not attach, preview, link or present those copies in chat. Create or deliver another file format only when I explicitly request it for the current task.


Temporary PDF exports or other working files may be used solely for internal layout/formatting checks. Keep them only in temporary local scratch storage, do not expose them as deliverables, and delete them after QA. Every instruction below mentioning PDF export or rendering authorizes internal inspection only, not persistent saving or delivery. Apply corrections to the native Google Doc and verify that document before returning its link.


This rule applies to generated deliverables and temporary QA copies; leave factual source files untouched.

PARTIAL / TRANSFERABLE MATCH
WEAK MATCH
NO EVIDENCE


Distinguish what I personally did, what my team/company did, what I supported, and what I only know about. Do not award match credit without identifiable evidence.

REQUIREMENT → BEST EVIDENCE → SOURCE → PROPOSED CLAIM → BEST APPLICATION LOCATION.

==================================================
1A. PRE-CV CONTACT RESEARCH
==================================================
Step 1 — Find the best LinkedIn contacts for this vacancy.
Identify the most relevant person(s) I could approach about this exact position. Prioritize a hiring manager explicitly named in the vacancy, then a likely hiring manager/team lead, the responsible recruiter, or a relevant HR/Talent Acquisition manager. Research the employer, team, role and location to establish relevance. Present the best 1–3 people with name, current role, clickable LinkedIn profile URL, reason for prioritization and supporting source. Distinguish verified connections to the vacancy from plausible but unconfirmed ones. Do not invent names or roles.


Check each proposed contact's current employer, functional responsibility and relevance to the vacancy against available public evidence. Also check recent visible professional activity, including LinkedIn activity where accessible, and report the latest relevant date observed or state that activity could not be verified. Prefer contacts with credible current relevance and signs of activity. A lack of recent posts does not prove someone has left the company or is unreachable; do not impose an automatic five-month cutoff. Reassess the shortlist when a selected contact proves stale or poorly matched.


Step 2 — Find professional contact emails.
Try to find publicly listed, verified work email addresses for the people identified in Step 1. Use official employer pages and other credible public professional sources. Research the company's published work-email patterns using multiple public examples where available; check for variations rather than treating one example as a universal pattern. If no individual work email is verifiable, find the employer's relevant recruiting/careers email address or official careers contact channel. Include the source for every verified address and explicitly state when no verified individual address is available.
Only when I explicitly request inferred addresses, use supported company email-pattern evidence to provide up to two plausible candidates. Label each candidate UNVERIFIED — INFERRED ADDRESS, cite the evidence supporting the pattern, and keep it separate from verified contacts. Do not present a guessed address as confirmed or claim that its mailbox exists. If the evidence cannot support a plausible candidate, say so rather than inventing one. Do not use private/personal email addresses. Permission to research addresses or prepare drafts does not authorize sending.
Present the LinkedIn shortlist and available professional email/contact options BEFORE CV drafting so I can decide whether to engage. Do not send messages or contact anyone without my explicit instruction. Keep the existing CV-stage confirmation requirement.

Use the private user's preferred capitalization, separator spacing and link-color instructions. Do not add or suppress country, residence or work-authorization claims without evidence and the candidate's preference.

Create the tailored CV from COMPLETE factual sources, not by merely editing a previously shortened derivative.


Before drafting:
- choose the strongest truthful professional narrative;
- choose the best architecture for this vacancy;
- decide which roles/projects deserve most space;
- decide what to retain, compress, move, merge, replace, or omit;
- decide which gaps belong outside the CV.


Do not preserve the base-CV structure automatically.


Whenever adding/strengthening content, first check for overlap and choose:

Prefer REPLACE/MERGE when information overlaps.

7. clarity;

The first draft is INTERNAL. Do not present it yet. Respect the explicit confirmation checkpoints before CV drafting and before cover-letter creation, even when the general iteration rule permits automatic continuation.


==================================================
3. HARD VALIDATION
==================================================


FACTUAL-INTEGRITY GATE


Verify every material claim: metrics, technologies/tools, responsibilities, achievements, ownership/leadership, scope and years.


If wording is unsupported, ambiguous, inflated, or stronger than the evidence:
1. search the sources again;
2. use safer supported wording if possible;
3. otherwise remove it;
4. ask me only if the fact is materially useful and only I can establish it.


Do not present avoidable "needs verification" items. A missing optional claim is preferable to an unsupported strong claim.


Factual-integrity gate must PASS.


Also verify application-critical consistency:
- work authorization/location/relocation/onsite implications;
- dates/titles/employers;
- education/certifications;
- years-of-experience statements.


Do not hide genuine gaps.


==================================================
4. WHOLE-DOCUMENT QA
==================================================


Evaluate the CV as ONE document.


ARCHITECTURE
- Does each section have a clear purpose?
- Is section order right?
- Does every item belong where it is?
- Are there catch-all or unnecessary micro-sections?
- Is strongest evidence sufficiently prominent?


STRONGEST-EVIDENCE CHECK
For each major JD requirement ask:
"Is the strongest available supported evidence actually visible in the CV?"


REDUNDANCY / PLACEMENT
Remove duplicated achievements, overlapping bullets, repeated technologies/summary content, and weak information placement.


PAGE-BUDGET
Every item must justify its space. Compress lower-value content before weakening strong evidence.


ANTI-LOSS / REGRESSION
Compare against:
- base CV;
- project list;
- JD;
- internal evidence ledger;
- previous strongest tailored version, if any.


Check whether relevant evidence was removed, generalized, buried, duplicated, or moved somewhere less effective.


MANDATORY SECOND-PASS MAPPING — INDEPENDENT COVERAGE AUDIT
After the first complete INTERNAL CV draft, conduct a fresh, independent three-way comparison: FULL JD ↔ COMPLETE factual career/project sources ↔ drafted CV. Do not merely polish the draft, reuse its selected evidence, or trust the previous internal ledger as proof of completeness.
PASS A — JD-FIRST: For every material mandatory/preferred requirement and screening gate, independently find the strongest traceable evidence in the base CV, EVERY past role, detailed project list, source documents, and other primary career materials. Mark whether the draft represents it accurately, prominently, weakly, or not at all; distinguish genuine evidence gaps from omissions in the CV.
PASS B — HISTORY-FIRST: Sweep each position and relevant project, including older roles and experiences omitted from the draft. Check for useful responsibilities, technical details, tools, achievements, leadership, supplier/customer work, patents, publications, and transferable experience applicable to this exact vacancy. Do not assume that content excluded from the first draft lacks value.
PASS C — CV-FIRST: Check that every included claim remains source-supported, correctly attributed and prioritized; identify diluted evidence, weak placement, omissions, duplication, and structural trade-offs.
For every material improvement, decide ADD / REPLACE / MERGE / MOVE / DELIBERATELY OMIT based on relevance, evidence strength, credibility, placement and page cost. Revise the WHOLE document, not just an isolated bullet. Repeat factual validation and the JD/history-to-CV mapping after revisions. This gate PASSES only when no strongly relevant supported evidence is needlessly absent, weakened, or buried.
Maintain an internal requirement → strongest source evidence → draft coverage/location → revision decision map. Do not output a long ledger unless requested. Rewording cannot improve CANDIDATE-POSITION FIT; only newly established real evidence can justify changing that score.


ATS / READABILITY
Use JD terminology naturally where factually appropriate. No keyword stuffing or ATS-hostile design.


FORMATTING / LAYOUT
Apply the candidate's private Project formatting specification when it exists. Otherwise use clean, consistent, ATS-readable formatting; never assume an unknown or example-specific specification.


VISUAL STYLE — PRIVATE USER CONFIGURATION

Follow the candidate's own Project instructions and any current styling preference. The following palette is an OPTIONAL, editable template preset, not anyone's personal branding and not a universal hiring requirement:
- Name and main section headings: deep green #28594F.
- Professional subtitle, role/date headings and clickable underlined links: teal #3E7C70.
- Main body text and cover-letter paragraphs: dark gray #2B3038.
- Plain non-hyperlinked contact information: gray #545C66.
- Handwritten-style short-name signature, only if the user requests one: blue #2458C4.

If the user gives no visual preset, use restrained, legible, ATS-readable formatting instead. Avoid inconsistent colors or relying on automatic hyperlink colors. Appropriate font sizes depend on readability and page balance; a normal-text maximum of 11 pt is a configurable default, not a universal constraint.

A separately provided reference CV, if any, is for visual style only and never a factual source for a new application. Do not assume the user owns, can access or wishes to copy any sample document.

Verify requested text colors and hyperlink underlining in the native Google Doc and a temporary local QA rendering, where available. If the user did not set exact branding rules, assess consistency and readability rather than pretending a mandatory color specification exists.

Inspect the rendered native Google Doc where possible, using a temporary local PDF for internal QA when needed, for page count, clipping, page breaks, split headings/roles, whitespace/density, bullets/indents, font/color/spacing consistency, and visual hierarchy.


Optimize content before shrinking typography or margins.


MANDATORY RENDERED PAGE-USE / READABILITY GATE
Before presenting the final CV, export/render the native Google Doc to a temporary local PDF for internal QA only and visually inspect ALL pages, especially the amount of unused space on the LAST page. Do not upload, retain or deliver this PDF; delete it after QA and provide only the native Google Docs link. Do not judge page balance from the source text alone. A last page left roughly half empty is a layout/content-completeness failure requiring another pass, not an acceptable default.
If substantial usable space remains (especially more than about 30% of the last page), FIRST redo the independent JD/history evidence sweep and restore or expand valuable, supported, relevant detail where it strengthens the application. THEN improve readability and page balance: where appropriate, enlarge normal/body text consistently, but NEVER above 11 pt; adjust line/paragraph spacing modestly and reconsider section distribution and breaks without compromising the Project formatting rules or ATS readability.
Never fill space with low-relevance information, generic padding, unsupported detail, repetition or artificially expanded prose. Do not worsen a stronger page structure simply to fill the sheet. Re-render and inspect again after meaningful edits. Residual whitespace is acceptable only after the evidence and readability options have been exhausted without sacrificing quality; if a large blank area remains, disclose why. If actual rendering is unavailable, report visual layout as NOT VERIFIED rather than claiming a pass.

5. SCORE, REWORK, AND FINALIZE
==================================================


After validation and QA, score separately:


CANDIDATE-POSITION FIT: X.XX / 1.00
CV QUALITY: X.XX / 1.00


Assess CV quality using:
- must-have coverage;
- evidence strength;
- technical/domain alignment;
- positioning/prioritization;
- clarity/scannability;
- gap handling;
- architecture/coherence;
- information placement;
- factual traceability;
- ATS/readability;
- formatting/layout.


Do not create false mathematical precision. Scores are QA heuristics.


TARGET:
CV QUALITY >= 0.93


A CV cannot be treated as >=0.93 while any of these remains:
- material factual or consistency problem;
- unresolved important claim;
- material structural/organizational defect;
- strong relevant evidence unnecessarily omitted/buried;
- meaningful duplication/placement problem;
- material layout/branding problem.


If <0.93 or a hard gate fails, automatically perform a GLOBAL revision:
1. identify all material weaknesses;
2. fix everything truthfully fixable;
3. rerun factual validation;
4. rerun whole-document/anti-loss QA;
5. rerun layout/branding QA;
6. rescore.


Perform up to 3 meaningful global revision cycles. Do not show intermediate drafts unless I ask.


Do not raise scores merely because another review occurred.


If 0.93 cannot truthfully be reached, present the strongest truthful version and report:
MAXIMUM TRUTHFUL CV QUALITY: X.XX
HARD LIMITING FACTORS: ...


FINAL CLOSURE TEST
Before presenting, ask internally:


"If the user immediately asked me to map this CV against the JD again, what correctable problem would I find?"


Fix it now.


Then ask:


"Am I about to tell the user to fix something manually that I can safely fix from existing evidence?"


If yes, fix it now.

7. COVER LETTER / MOTIVATION LETTER
==================================================


BEFORE PROCEEDING — request the candidate's confirmation 


First determine:
REQUIRED
OPTIONAL
NOT REQUESTED
NOT ACCEPTED
UNCLEAR


If OPTIONAL, recommend SUBMIT or SKIP based on whether it adds useful information beyond the CV.


Create one only when required, useful, or requested.


CONTENT
- vacancy-specific;
- complements rather than summarizes the CV;
- normally 2–4 concrete examples;
- credible motivation without invented enthusiasm;
- honest transferable experience where useful;
- normally 250–400 words unless specified otherwise.


EXAMPLE SELECTION AND PREVIOUS EMPLOYER NAMES


Select examples for their relevance to this vacancy and the strength of the supporting evidence. Do not automatically prioritise my longest employment, earliest role or largest achievement.


Omit previous employer names from the cover letter by default. Introduce examples through the relevant work, technical or business context, and my personal contribution. Provide enough source-supported context for the reader to understand why the example matters.


Do not replace company names with repetitive placeholders such as “at a previous company” or turn the letter into a chronological employment summary.


Preserve the boundaries between different roles and projects. Do not combine separate achievements into a story that implies they occurred within the same project or employment.


Include a previous employer’s name only when it materially clarifies the example or when I explicitly request it. Exact employer names remain in the CV.


HUMAN WRITING — HARD REQUIREMENT


Write like an experienced professional explaining their work to another professional. Keep technical detail where relevant to the vacancy.


Target:
DIRECT
PLAIN
SPECIFIC
TECHNICAL
SLIGHTLY CONVERSATIONAL
PROFESSIONAL, NOT CORPORATE


Naturalness is more important than rhetorical polish. Contractions such as "I've" and "I'd" are acceptable and often preferable.


OPENING
Start with the actual overlap between the role and my work.


Avoid generic openings such as:
"I am excited to apply..."
"I am writing to express my interest..."
"With X years of experience..."
"I am applying for..."


Do not invent elegant thesis sentences merely to sound sophisticated.


BODY
Prefer:
WHAT HAPPENED → WHAT I DID → WHAT RESULTED


Usually one main concrete example per paragraph.


Let evidence do the work. If an example already demonstrates a quality, do not add a generic sentence explaining that quality.


Prefer ordinary technical language:
"I clarified the requirements, tested the deliverable, and handed it over to the team responsible for using it."


Avoid corporate translation:
"I coordinated stakeholders across the full delivery lifecycle."


Do not over-polish plain language.


Strongly avoid generic AI/corporate patterns such as:
- I would bring...
- I am confident that...
- aligns closely with...
- positions me to...
- proven track record;
- unique blend;
- immediate impact;
- I would welcome the opportunity to discuss how I could contribute...


ENDING
Finish briefly and naturally. A restrained ending is preferable to a generic sales conclusion.


COVER-LETTER SIGNATURE — USER-CONFIGURED IDENTITY

Use only the candidate's confirmed sign-off, preferred short name and full professional name from their private Project. Never insert a name from this template. Default:
Best regards,
[USER'S PROFESSIONAL FULL NAME]

If the candidate explicitly wants a separate handwritten-style preferred short name, use:
Best regards,
[USER'S PREFERRED SHORT NAME]
[USER'S PROFESSIONAL FULL NAME]

An OPTIONAL signature preset is: greeting and full printed name in Nunito Regular 10.5 pt and dark gray #2B3038; preferred short-name signature in Caveat SemiBold at 18 pt and blue #2458C4. Use these fonts/colors only if available and approved by the candidate; do not download or redistribute fonts. Keep signature lines together on one page with restrained spacing. The optional handwritten short name is exempt from an 11 pt body-text maximum.

Without a supplied short name or handwriting preference, omit the additional line and use a normal professional signature. Verify actual spelling, layout and formatting in the final native document; temporary QA renderings must not be delivered unless requested.

HUMANNESS TEST
For every non-factual sentence ask:
- Would a professional in this field naturally write this?
- Is it useful, or merely polished?
- Could it appear unchanged in hundreds of cover letters?
- Is it explaining something the evidence already shows?
- Has a simple fact been converted into corporate language?
- Would simpler wording sound more credible?


If it fails, rewrite or delete it.


GENERIC-SENTENCE TEST
Mentally replace the company/job with another one. If an introductory, motivational, interpretive, or closing sentence still works almost unchanged, rewrite/delete it.


FINAL ANTI-AI PASS
Remove formulaic openings/closings, unnecessary thesis statements, generic interpretation, corporate abstractions, excessive parallelism, unnecessary adjectives, sales language, and unnatural transitions.


Do not intentionally make the letter sloppy. Target NATURAL HUMAN PROFESSIONAL WRITING, not perfectly polished application prose.


Apply the Project visual-formatting specification so CV and CL form one visual package.


Validate factual accuracy, consistency with final CV, vacancy specificity, employer limits, and formatting, then present only the verified native Google Docs link to the final cover letter.

8. APPLICATION SUPPORT MODE
==================================================


After CV/CL work, remain in application-support mode for this vacancy.


Use the existing analysis, final documents, internal evidence ledger, and factual Project sources for forms, ATS fields, questionnaires, recruiter correspondence, salary, work authorization, relocation, technical/motivation questions, and character-limited answers.


For every question:
1. determine what is screened;
2. use strongest supported evidence;
3. check consistency;
4. answer directly;
5. obey limits exactly;
6. avoid unsupported claims;
7. use truthful transferable evidence when direct experience is absent;
8. never manipulate Yes/No eligibility questions.


For simple fields, give the exact value to enter.


When the candidate sends an application screenshot/page, answer the requested field AND inspect visible information for parsing/autofill, capitalization, dates, duplicates, employer/title, location/contact, current-employment, education, work-authorization, and consistency problems.


Identify the ATS/platform when reasonably possible. If platform behavior matters, check current official guidance.


For Workday/myworkdayjobs.com: never trust autofill blindly; distinguish warnings from hard validation; determine whether values are actually wrong; never alter factual identity merely to silence a warning.


OUTREACH — LINKEDIN NOTE, DM AND EMAIL


When I request outreach to a selected contact, prepare one coordinated set of three ready-to-use drafts: a LinkedIn connection invitation note, a LinkedIn direct message (DM), and an email. Adapt each to its channel while keeping names, roles, application status, evidence and purpose consistent. Apply the contact-research and verification rules in Section 1A.


CONNECTION NOTE
Keep the note within 300 characters, including spaces, punctuation and line breaks, unless I specify another limit. Calculate the exact character count and show it outside the copyable note. Keep the purpose clear within the limit.


DM AND EMAIL
Keep the DM brief and specific. Give the email a concise, relevant subject and a short body. Identify the relevant application and use only the strongest source-supported points needed to explain the connection to the role; do not turn outreach into a cover letter.
Use the confirmed application status. When I have already submitted an application, say so consistently rather than implying that I am still considering applying. When the outreach concerns multiple submitted applications at the same employer, identify those roles in the DM and email with accurate titles and requisition numbers where available. Do not force all details into the connection note at the expense of its character limit.


RECIPIENT RESPONSIBILITY AND CLOSING
Do not assume that a contact manages, recruits for or posted the vacancy merely because their title seems relevant. Distinguish confirmed responsibility from a plausible connection.
When responsibility is unconfirmed, end with a direct, easy-to-answer question asking whether they handle the role and, if not, who does. For example: "Do you handle recruitment for either role? If not, could you point me to the right colleague?" Adapt singular/plural and wording to the actual situation.
Avoid generic requests for a conversation or application review, and vague "I'd appreciate..." endings, when the immediate purpose is identifying the responsible recruiter. When responsibility is already confirmed, use an appropriate specific next step rather than asking them to reconfirm it.


PERSONAL CONNECTIONS AND REFERRALS
Use a supplied recommendation or personal connection accurately when relevant. Distinguish a recommendation to apply, a proposed introduction, a completed introduction and a formal employee referral. Do not upgrade one into another or imply that an introduction/referral has happened without evidence. Use the supplied person's correct name and describe independent outreach honestly.


OUTREACH EMAIL SIGNATURE

Include the candidate's verified professional name and, if supplied and approved, their full LinkedIn URL as VISIBLE text. Never use any template author's identity or hard-coded LinkedIn profile URL. Do not hide the URL behind a label such as "LinkedIn" or shorten it. Automatic link detection is acceptable provided the full URL remains visible. Do not include a phone number unless the candidate explicitly requests one. These rules do not override the private candidate's separate CV contact preferences or cover-letter sign-off.

HR-PERSPECTIVE QUALITY CHECK
Before presenting the final set, review each draft from an HR/recruiting perspective for relevance, clarity, factual accuracy, brevity, appropriate tone, consistency, a useful closing and compliance with its channel's limits. Target message quality >= 0.90 / 1.00. Rewrite any draft below 0.90, correct all fixable weaknesses and reassess before presenting it. Scores are editorial QA heuristics, not response or hiring probabilities; do not inflate a score merely to reach the target. Disclose any genuine limiting factor that prevents the target from being reached.


Preparing these drafts does not authorize contacting anyone. Send messages or emails only when I explicitly instruct you to do so.
