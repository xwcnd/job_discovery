# Structured career-evidence enrichment (optional but recommended)

A CV and a project list are the initial factual sources, not a guarantee that every relevant skill is documented at the right depth. This process elicits **additional true information from the applicant** without turning plausible experience into invented credentials. It generalizes the source workflow's interactive, project-by-project evidence questionnaire.

## When to use it

Use when a promising job exposes an important factual unknown; when a detailed CV lists generic methods without demonstrating where/how they were used; when career evidence is old or incomplete; or when a user explicitly asks to improve the source record. **Do not restart an already completed questionnaire**. Locate its last unanswered section and continue there. Participation is optional, and a person may answer partially.

## Sequence (one stage at a time)

1. **Evidence inventory.** Read the current base CV, original project list, other primary records, and any prior answers. Note already established role periods, employer names, scope, methods, results and known gaps. Ask only for material missing facts.
2. **Functional activity inventory.** Explore role-relevant activities through plain-language choices: personally designed/implemented, led a team doing it, coordinated a supplier, tested or supported, observed/learned, or no experience. Specify relevant environments (laboratory, manufacturing, field/service, software/business), technologies and independently performed tasks.
3. **Project mapping.** Assign each claimed activity to an exact role/project/time period. Ask what the person actually did, for what technical object/customer, with whom, which decision authority, which documents and tests, which outcome, and how it was measured. Broad self-reported skills should **not** automatically become hands-on experience in every project.
4. **Method-name verification.** Distinguish officially trained/certified use of a named method from practical activities that resemble it. For example, an applicant may have used root-cause diagrams and iterative improvement without completing a formal Lean Six Sigma certification or naming every practice. Credit the real actions, not assumed formal status.
5. **Ownership and result attribution.** Separate `I performed`, `I led/coordinated`, `I contributed`, `my team/company delivered`, and `I only learned/observed`. Confirm whether numerical results are personally measured, team-reported, estimated or absent. Record measurement method and baseline if known; do not turn a team result into individual ownership.
6. **Evidence and contradictions.** Record first-person statements with date and anchored project; compare with primary documents; flag conflicting metrics, periods, roles or techniques rather than picking the stronger wording. Confidential project details should be described at a permitted high level only.
7. **Source update decision.** Show a concise factual correction/addition draft and how it affects relevant job screens. Edit the controlling CV or any external record **only after explicit authorization**. A questionnaire answer is new evidence, not automatic approval to rewrite a full document.

## Good project-level questions

Use small batches and plain-language definitions, not a long interrogatory. Examples:

- In which exact job/project did you use this technique, and was it in a real production operation, pilot, laboratory or software process?
- Did you personally perform it, lead others, specify a supplier's work, or merely review the results?
- What was the real deliverable: equipment, validated method, SOP, requirements, report, installation, process change or commercial decision?
- Were the steps formally named/trained/accredited, or functionally similar without formal use of the name?
- What exactly changed, how was it tested, who measured the result, and is the number from a documented or estimated source?
- What important task did you **not** do, despite being on the same project?

Unknown is a legitimate result. Do not repeatedly ask answered questions. If an answer disqualifies an existing tracked job, re-assess that entire vacancy and reconcile the live private tracker; preserve the correction separately in private audit.
